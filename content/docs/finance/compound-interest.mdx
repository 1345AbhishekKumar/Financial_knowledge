---
title: Compound interest
description: Why starting early beats saving more — the math behind “money makes money”.
---

Compound interest is interest earned on interest. Each period, your balance
grows — and the next period's growth is calculated on the **new, larger**
balance. Time does the heavy lifting.

## The formula

```text
A = P × (1 + r/n) ^ (n × t)

P = starting amount      r = annual rate
n = compounds per year   t = years
```

## Worked example

$1,000 invested once at 7% annual return, compounded yearly:

| Year | Balance |
| ---- | ------- |
| 0 | $1,000.00 |
| 5 | $1,402.55 |
| 10 | $1,967.15 |
| 20 | $3,869.68 |
| 30 | $7,612.26 |

<Callout type="info">
  Nothing was added after year zero. The extra $6,612 is purely the
  balance compounding on itself.
</Callout>

## Compute it yourself

```js
function compound(principal, rate, compoundsPerYear, years) {
  return principal * (1 + rate / compoundsPerYear) ** (compoundsPerYear * years);
}

console.log(compound(1000, 0.07, 1, 30).toFixed(2));
// "7612.26"
```

## Starting early vs saving more

| Saver | Monthly | Years | Total paid in | Final balance* |
| ----- | ------- | ----- | ------------- | -------------- |
| Early | $200 | 40 | $96,000 | ≈ $525,000 |
| Late | $400 | 20 | $96,000 | ≈ $245,000 |

\*At 7% annual return. Same money in, wildly different money out —
that gap is compounding.
