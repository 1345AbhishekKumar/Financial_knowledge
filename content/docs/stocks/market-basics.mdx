Buying a share means buying a fractional claim on a business's future cash
flows. Everything else — the ticker, the chart, the "price" — is a summary of
what thousands of people currently believe those flows are worth.

## What you own

| Shareholder right | What it means |
| ----------------- | ------------- |
| Residual claim | Paid last, after bondholders and creditors |
| Voting right | One vote per share on board and major decisions |
| Dividend entitlement | A share of distributions the board declares |
| Liquidation right | A claim on remaining assets if the firm winds up |

<Callout type="info">
  The legal claim is small and constant. The value is entirely in the cash
  flows you expect the business to produce.
</Callout>

## How a price forms

A price is the point where the marginal buyer and marginal seller agree. Behind
that number sit two forces:

1. **Intrinsic value** — cash flows discounted back at an appropriate rate
2. **Market sentiment** — what everyone else believes today

The gap between them is the entire field of market study.

## Order types

| Order | Instruction | Use when |
| ----- | ----------- | -------- |
| Market | Buy now at any available price | You need the fill, not the price |
| Limit | Buy only at your price or better | You refuse to overpay |
| Stop | Becomes a market order at a trigger | You need to cut a loss automatically |

## Compute it yourself

```js
// Intrinsic value as the present value of 3 years of cash flow
// plus a terminal value (here: a perpetuity growing at g).
function intrinsicValue(cf, rate, growth) {
  const pv = cf.reduce((sum, flow, i) => sum + flow / (1 + rate) ** i, 0);
  const terminal = cf[cf.length - 1] * (1 + growth) / (rate - growth);
  return pv + terminal / (1 + rate) ** cf.length;
}

console.log(intrinsicValue([100, 100, 100], 0.08, 0.03).toFixed(2));
```

<Callout type="warn">
  A single discount rate assumption drives the entire answer. Treat any
  intrinsic value as a range, never a precise figure.
</Callout>
