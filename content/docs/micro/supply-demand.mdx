Supply and demand is the workhorse model of microeconomics. Demand describes
what buyers want at each price; supply describes what sellers offer. Where they
meet, the market clears.

## The curves

| Price | Quantity demanded | Quantity supplied | Result |
| ----- | ---------------- | ---------------- | ------ |
| $10 | 100 | 40 | Shortage of 60 → price rises |
| $6 | 60 | 60 | **Equilibrium** |
| $3 | 20 | 85 | Surplus of 65 → price falls |

At equilibrium, quantity demanded equals quantity supplied — no pressure for
the price to move.

## Shifting vs moving along

The most common mistake is moving along a curve when you meant to shift it.

- **Movement along** — a change in the good's own price
- **Shift of the curve** — anything *other* than its own price

<Callout type="info">
  Rainfall, taxes, consumer income, the price of inputs, and the number of
  sellers all *shift* curves. The good's own price never does.
</Callout>

## Shifters

| Curve shifts right when… | Because… |
| ----------------------- | -------- |
| Consumer income rises (normal good) | More demand at every price |
| Input costs fall | Production is cheaper at every output |
| More sellers enter | More supply at every price |
| A substitute gets pricier | Buyers switch toward this good |

## Compute the new equilibrium

Two linear curves. Change a shifter, solve for the crossing point again.

```js
// demand: Qd = a - b·P     supply: Qs = c + d·P
function equilibrium(demand, supply) {
  // solve a - b·P = c + d·P  →  P = (a - c) / (b + d)
  const price = (demand.a - supply.c) / (demand.b + supply.d);
  const quantity = demand.a - demand.b * price;
  return { price, quantity };
}

console.log(equilibrium({ a: 120, b: 10 }, { c: 0, d: 10 }));
// { price: 6, quantity: 60 }
```

Run it again with `{ a: 150, b: 10 }` — demand shifted right, and you get a
higher price **and** a higher quantity traded.

## Tax and deadweight loss

A per-unit tax of $2 wedges the two curves apart:

| Price | Buyer pays | Seller receives |
| ----- | ---------- | --------------- |
| Before tax | $6 | $6 |
| After tax | $7 | $5 |

Total tax revenue is $2 × quantity — and the volume that no longer trades is
the **deadweight loss**.

<Callout type="warn">
  Tax revenue is not the cost of a tax. The deadweight loss — the trades that
  stop happening at all — is the part with no offsetting benefit.
</Callout>
